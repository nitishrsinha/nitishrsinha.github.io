How papers are selected

Economics and finance categories (econ.EM, econ.GN, econ.TH, q-fin.CP, q-fin.EC, q-fin.GN, q-fin.PM, q-fin.RM, q-fin.ST, q-fin.TR): every new paper is screened.

Computer science categories (cs.CY, cs.GT, cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.MA, cs.LG): papers that mention both an AI term and an economics or finance term.

AI terms: artificial intelligence, generative ai, genai, large language model, llm, machine learning, ai agent, foundation model, chatgpt, automation, ai adoption, frontier model, language model, ai system.

Economics and finance terms: labor market, labour market, wages, employment, productivity gains, economic growth, economic impact, economics of, market concentration, market power, pricing, inequality, welfare, antitrust, monopoly, gdp, macroeconomic, occupations, economy, economic, workers, labor, labour, consumer surplus, auction, market design, financial markets, market efficiency, event study, financial, finance, stock market, stock returns, asset pricing, asset prices, portfolio, trading, investors, banking, credit, monetary policy, central bank, inflation, markets, firms, regulation, policy, incentives, demand, supply, forecasting, price, prices, risk.

AI screening: candidate papers are then scored for relevance to the economics and finance of AI by an AI language model, which also writes the one-sentence summaries. Papers scored as not relevant are dropped, so the one-line summaries are machine-generated and may contain errors; read the abstract for anything you rely on. The model currently in use is claude-opus-5-5; earlier entries may have been screened by a different model.
